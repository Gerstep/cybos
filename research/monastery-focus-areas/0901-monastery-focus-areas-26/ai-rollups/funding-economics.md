## 3. Buy-side multiples for services SMEs

**Accounting** (most PE-penetrated vertical): platform-grade (35%+ CAS/advisory mix) → **9–13x EBITDA** (1.3–1.6x revenue); compliance-only/founder-dependent → **4–6x EBITDA** (0.8–1.1x revenue). PE holds stakes in 11 of the top 30 US firms, >$50B invested over 6 years (KPMG data).

**Cross-vertical bolt-on ranges, 2026** (CT Acquisitions): HVAC 5–7x · plumbing 4–6x · roofing 4–7x · pest control 5–8x · dental DSOs 5–9x · vet 8–15x · eye care 6–10x · auto repair 4–7x · **IT services/MSP 5–9x** · **accounting 4–8x** (bolt-on) / 8–13x (platform).

**Arbitrage mechanics**: buy platform at 10x EBITDA ($5M EBITDA → $50M EV) + 5 bolt-ons at 5x avg ($25M total) = $75M invested. Exit combined $10M EBITDA at 11x = $110M EV → **$35M value creation before any organic growth or margin improvement.**

## 4. Claimed vs. observed margin expansion — the open question

**The core GC thesis**: 5–10% EBITDA (traditional services) → 30–40% EBITDA (software-like), via AI automating 30–70% of workflows across 4 task categories (support/comms, data entry, content gen, early-stage reasoning).

**Company claims (all self-reported, unaudited)**:
- Long Lake: **$100M EBITDA in <2 years**; organic growth **0–5%→20%+/yr** (CEO, No Priors podcast, May 2026)
- Crescendo: **4x margin advantage** vs. traditional BPO (10–15% typical); **>$100M ARR in <2 years**; ~90% autonomous resolution
- Beacon Software: **>50% EBITDA growth**/yr; **~1,000bps average Rule-of-40 improvement** within 1 year of acquisition
- Accrual/Current: 6hrs saved on simple returns, up to 85% time-cut on complex cases; Current's Tax AI processed 7,000 returns, 31% avg time savings
- Dwelly: manager productivity **150→600+ properties/person**
- Adaptive Innovations: **-80%** clinician documentation time; **<5% vs. 11%** industry rehospitalization rate

**Independent skepticism**:
- Capital & Clarity (independent analyst): *"margin transformation numbers are reported by the firms themselves, not independently audited."*
- **AI Roll-up Nexus investor survey**: **79%** of active roll-up investors flag Integration/Change Management as top risk; **68%** flag "Overhyped AI value-creation" — the funders themselves are skeptical at these rates.
- "The AI Roll-Up Trap" (demg.ai): *"General Catalyst's AI roll-up portfolio is under three years old. None of the portfolio companies have been through a recession... Treat the thesis as a hypothesis to verify, not a conclusion to accept."*
    50|- Dave Friedman critique: classic roll-up failure modes (overpaying, integration complexity, culture resistance, debt fragility, messy data) aren't solved by AI: *"If you believe AI eliminates integration debt, you are essentially claiming that this time gravity runs upward."*
- **Monastery read: every specific margin/EBITDA number above is a sponsor or company claim, not a verified, audited, multi-year, recession-tested outcome.**

## 7. Debt vs. equity structures — the stack changes with scale

**Small/early (equity-only)**: Titan's $74M funded the RFA acquisition with no disclosed debt. Eudia's $105M = $30M upfront equity + $75M released conditionally per-acquisition (GC governance substitutes for lender covenants).

**Mid-scale (blended)**: Dwelly = £32M equity + £37M debt (**debt ≈ 54%** of raise), secured against acquired agencies' cash flows.

**Billion-dollar scale (full LBO stack, identical to traditional PE)**:
- **Amex GBT/Long Lake ($6.3B)**: equity from Long Lake's investor base + fully committed debt from JPMorgan, BofA, Citi, MUFG — **"not subject to any financing condition."**
- **Janus Henderson/Trian+GC ($6.5B)**: Trian/GC equity + MassMutual preferred equity + **8-bank debt syndicate** (JPMorgan, Citi, BofA, Jefferies, MUFG, SMBC, UBS, Morgan Stanley) — textbook large-cap LBO stack.
- **Metropolis/SP+**: shifted from $1.05B preferred + $550M debt (2024) to **$1.1B senior secured loan vs. only $500M equity** (Nov 2025) as EBITDA proved out — sponsors lever up exactly like any cash-flowing LBO asset once cash flow is real.

**Tension**: GC claims *"we rely less on debt funding... than traditional PE."* True at the venture-check stage (Titan, Eudia, Accrual = equity-only) — **false at billion-dollar scale**, where GC's own flagship crossover deals (Amex GBT, Janus Henderson) use full multi-bank debt syndicates indistinguishable from traditional take-privates.

## 8. Why a $2M Monastery check cannot buy a roll-up platform — the math

1. **Every entry-stage round is 25–50x+ a Monastery check**: Crescendo $50M (25x) · Titan $74M (37x) · Eudia $105M (52x) · Accrual $75M (37x) · Dwelly ~$93M (46x) · Sequence Holdings' lean $75M (37x, the smallest credible example in the entire dataset).
2. **At bolt-on multiples (4–9x EBITDA), $2M buys only a ~$220K–$500K-EBITDA business** — solo-practitioner scale, not a platform — and it would need to be 100% cash, since Monastery-stage founders have no collateral or track record to access the acquisition debt every roll-up above used to stretch its equity. Nothing is left for AI build, integration, or a second deal.
3. **The GC/Thrive playbook requires "build AI" and "acquire" capital simultaneously or in sequence** — Titan, Eudia, Accrual all had product-build capital before/alongside their first acquisition; Thrive spent ~2 years building Crete before OpenAI's equity investment scaled it. $2M spent on either step leaves nothing for the other.
4. **Regulatory/licensing overhead compounds this**: CPA alternative practice structures, home-health state licensure, bank-regulatory approval (Sequence Holdings/BankSouth needed a full Latham & Watkins team across three regulatory workstreams) — not executable by a 2-person team in 12 weeks alongside product work.

**Bottom line: $2M buys a "roll-up of one," not a platform.** No example in this dataset launched credibly on $2M or less; the observed range is $50M–$670M+ before the third acquisition.

## 9. What $2M CAN fund — the Monastery-fit alternative

- **Build the automation layer first, on someone else's balance sheet.** Long Lake's Nexus, Titan's Augmented AI, Eudia's Company Brain, Accrual's ingestion engine — all started as standalone software validated against real client workflows *before* the acquisition capital arrived. A Monastery team can prove the same 30–70% automation claim via a revenue-share/license deal with a design-partner services business, with zero ownership required, inside 12 weeks.
- **Become the acquisition target or exclusive tooling vendor**, not a competing roll-up. Titan (20+ deals/yr target), Beacon (~1 deal/week), Current, and Shield Technology Partners are all actively sourcing exactly this kind of pre-built AI tooling to bolt on.
- **Split the raise like every scaled team did**: a $2–5M product/pilot tranche now (comparable to Accrual's initial $16M or Titan's pre-RFA seed), then a **separate** $50M–500M+ acquisition-vehicle raise later, once automation is proven with real numbers — not one check trying to do both.
- **Sell the automation into the SME base instead of owning the SMEs** ("arm the rebels," Current's model for independent firms) — pure SaaS/services-software Series Seed economics, no acquisition capital needed, fits Monastery's check size and 12-week timeline exactly.
