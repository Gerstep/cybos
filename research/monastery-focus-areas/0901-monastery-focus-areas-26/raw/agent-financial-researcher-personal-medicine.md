## SECTION 4: TAKE-PRIVATES AND FAILURES (2022–2024)

### One Medical → Amazon (Take-Private / Acquisition)
- **History**: Founded 2007. IPO Jan 2020 (Nasdaq: ONEM). Peak valuation ~$7B.
- **Amazon acquisition**: Closed Feb 2023 at **$3.9B** (18x revenue; ~180x EBITDA — was unprofitable)
- **Post-acquisition**: One Medical had **$420M operating loss in 2022** (last disclosed). Amazon Project Espresso (2024) ordered $100M+ in cost cuts.
- **Projected 2024 operating loss**: $500M+; hundreds of layoffs, office closures.
- **Pattern**: Premium membership concierge ($199/yr + insurance billing). High CAC, sophisticated patients, slow unit-level economics. Sold at a strategic premium for Amazon Prime Health integration.
- Source: [businessinsider.com](https://www.businessinsider.com/amazon-one-medical-project-espresso-reset-amazon-healthcare-bet-2024-2) (Feb 2024)

### Forward Health (Shutdown)
- **Founded**: 2016. Total raised: **$650M+**. Last round: $100M Series E (Nov 2023).
- **Shutdown**: **November 2024** — abrupt closure of all clinics and app.
- **Model**: AI-powered CarePod kiosks ($149/mo membership). Hardware-first clinics ($2–3M each). In-house lab tests.
- **Failure causes**:
  - Technical failures: automated blood draws broke repeatedly; key lab tests pulled
  - Unit economics never worked: high clinic buildout cost, expensive CAC, low retention
  - Burned $650M, ran out of runway <1 year after Series E
- **Lesson**: Hardware + clinic roll-out is capital-hungry; unit economics must hold before scale
- Source: [businessinsider.com](https://www.businessinsider.com/healthcare-startup-forward-shutdown-carepod-adrian-aoun-2024-11)

### Babylon Health (Bankruptcy)
- **Founded**: 2013 (UK). SPAC IPO Oct 2021 @ $4.1B valuation.
- **Bankruptcy**: Chapter 7 filing Aug 2023. $1.2B+ total raised.
- **Model**: AI symptom checker + virtual primary care. Value-based care contracts (US). UK NHS contracts.
- **Failure causes**:
  - SPAC listing set unrealistic revenue expectations
  - Expanded into US clinical delivery before tech was mature
  - High patient acquisition costs; reimbursement below projections
  - Take-private deal with MindMaze collapsed
- Source: [fiercehealthcare.com](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/babylon-closes-us-business-lays-employees-after-mindmaze-take-private-deal-collapses)

### Cerebral (Regulatory Near-Death, Survived)
- **Founded**: 2019. Total raised: **$462M**. Peak valuation: **$4.8B** (2021).
- **Model**: Telehealth ADHD/mental health prescribing. Mass-market.
- **Regulatory crisis**:
  - DEA investigation: encouraged overprescription of Adderall/stimulants 2019–2022
  - Internal metric: target "near 100%" of ADHD patients on stimulants; internal "prescription rate" KPI
  - DOJ settlement (Nov 2024): **$6.57M** (NPA agreement; most penalties deferred)
  - FTC settlement (Apr 2024): **$7M** — privacy violations, cancellation deception
  - HIPAA breach Jan 2023: 3.2M patients' data shared to Facebook/Google/TikTok pixel trackers
- **Stopped controlled substances**: Oct 2022 (entirely). Mass layoffs and restructuring.
- **Status as of 2025–26**: Surviving; pivoted to insurance model; first acquisition (Aug 2025). Valuation substantially below $4.8B peak.
- Source: [bhbusiness.com](https://bhbusiness.com/2024/11/05/cerebral-hit-with-6-6m-penalty-in-settlement-with-prosecutors/) (Nov 2024)

## SECTION 5: TYPICAL SEED–A RANGES BY BUSINESS MODEL

### Cash-Pay Concierge Clinic (Single Location or Network)
- **Seed**: $2M–$8M @ $8M–$20M pre-money. Typically friends/family + early HLS angels.
- **Series A**: $10M–$30M @ $40M–$80M. Once 2–3 locations profitable.
- **Unit model**: $3,200/yr avg fee × 300–500 members/physician = $960k–$1.6M per-physician revenue. 35–50% overhead. Physician takes ~$400–600k. Hard to scale without franchise/brand.
- **Hybrid concierge + insurance**: Lower fees ($1,500–$2,500/yr), larger panels (600–1,200), more scalable physician economics.

### AI-Native Membership / DTC Lab Marketplace
- **Seed**: $5M–$20M @ $20M–$60M pre-money. AI premium +42% over non-AI peers (Carta 2024).
- **Series A**: $20M–$50M @ $60M–$200M. Superpower: $30M @ $300M (AI premium to the extreme).
- **Key unit economics**: CAC $50–$200 (digital DTC). Annual fee $199–$2,500. Payback at first renewal; LTV/CAC 5–20x if retention >70%.
- **Example**: Function @ $365/yr × 500k members = ~$182M membership ARR + imaging upside. $2.5B valuation = ~13–14x ARR (premium for growth rate + mission narrative).

### Peptide / Compounding Telehealth Platform
- **Seed**: $3M–$16M. Mostly 2025–2026 activity; sector still early.
- **Series A**: $15M–$40M (if FDA regulatory clarity materializes).
- **Revenue model**: Membership ($60/mo = $720/yr) + peptide sales ($200/vial, 8–12 vials/yr = $1,600–$2,400/yr per active member). Combined ARPU: $2,300–$3,100/member/yr.
- **Risk**: Entire sector depends on FDA Advisory Committee outcomes (July 2026) and ongoing 503A/503B rulemaking. 12+ peptides removed from Category 2 (Apr 2026); 7 more under July 2026 PCAC review.

### Mental Health / Therapy / Coaching Platform
- **Seed**: $2M–$10M. Many pre-2023 deals underperformed.
- **Series A**: $15M–$50M. Now bifurcated:
  - Consumer therapy apps: under pressure (Cerebral cautionary tale)
  - B2B behavioral health infra (Ease Health $41M Series A): strong a16z backing
  - Payer-side analytics (Onos $17M Series A): niche but sticky
- **Unit economics**: Consumer therapy $80–$150/session, or $40–$80/mo subscription. B2B SaaS per-provider or PMPM contracts ($3–8 PMPM). High churn in consumer; stickier in employer benefits.

### Insurance Claims vs Cash Panel Model
- **Insurance claims model** (Prosper, classic concierge hybrid):
  - Lower sticker price; broader addressable market; insurance covers procedures
  - Gross margin ~25–40% (lab claims, visit claims thin after cost)
  - Predictable PMPM economics; stickier employer contracts
  - **Reference**: Progyny (employer fertility benefits) — 25.5% gross margin, $1.3B revenue, $200M+ operating cash
- **Cash-pay model** (Function, Neko, Superpower):
  - Higher sticker price; self-selects wealthy/health-conscious early adopter
  - Gross margin 55–75% (mostly SaaS/lab coordination cost)
  - CAC lower for organic/waitlist brands; no payer negotiation
  - **Reference**: Hims & Hers — 59% revenue growth 2025, +$128M net income, $2.35B revenue; FCF margin +33%

## SECTION 8: MONASTERY MATH ($2M / 5% → ~$40M POST-MONEY)

**Monastery terms**: $2M uncapped SAFE + 5% equity = implied ~$40M post-money valuation.

### What fits at $40M post:
1. **AI-native DTC lab orchestration startup** — pre-product or early traction. Quest/Labcorp white-label; AI-guided interpretation. Comparable: Superpower pre-seed ($4M, 2024, before $300M+ valuation). Mito Health (YC, $4M total, $2M seed, Mar 2025).
2. **Peptide/compounding coordination platform** — clinician matching + protocol engine + 503A pharmacy network. Protocole raised $6M seed (Apr 2026) → $40M post is slightly above but possible pre-revenue.
3. **AI concierge primary care** — technology layer, not physician employer. Prosper raised $16M seed (Jul 2026), implying much higher post ($40–60M range given FUSE lead). $40M Monastery fit = pre-Prosper stage.
4. **Specialist telehealth** — fertility (Progyny model), mental health (therapy + AI coaching), nutrition. Smaller TAM focus; $40M post reasonable for pre-revenue single-specialty.
5. **Longevity clinic tech (single market)** — Biopeak ($3M follow-on, Jan 2026, $7M total), Mito Health ($4M total), Biolytica ($6M Series A). These are the right scale.

### What doesn't fit (too big or too capital-intensive):
- Function Health ($800M+ raised), Neko Health ($1B+ raised), Superpower post-Series A ($300M+) — too large
- Physical clinic roll-outs requiring $2–3M per location (Forward Health trap)
- Compounding-only plays with regulatory risk requiring large pharmacy buildout
- Insurance-billed primary care networks (One Medical never worked at lean capital; requires >$50M)

### The Monastery opportunity:
**Best fit**: An AI-native software/coordination layer on top of existing infrastructure (Quest/Labcorp draws, 503A pharmacies, telehealth physicians), enabling personalized care delivery without owning clinics. Capture value through subscription + marketplace commission. $2M buys 6–12 months of product + clinical validation; 12 weeks of Monastery builds AI differentiation.

## SECTION 9: KEY REGULATORY CONTEXT

### Compounding / Peptides
- GLP-1 (semaglutide/tirzepatide): FDA shortage list → compounding legal → FDA declared shortage resolved early 2025 → compounding largely halted → Hims & Hers headwinds. Litigation ongoing.
- Peptides: FDA moved 17 peptides to Category 2 (banned from compounding, late 2023). RFK Jr. / MAHA agenda signaled deregulation. FDA removed 12 from Category 2 (Apr 2026). PCAC July 23–24, 2026 reviewed 7 more (BPC-157, TB-500, KPV, etc.). Advisory vote favorable; notice-and-comment rulemaking may add 12+ months.
- 503A (physician-prescribing pharmacies) vs 503B (outsourcing facilities): Very different regulatory burden. Most startups target 503A.
- Source: [thepeptidecatalog.com](https://thepeptidecatalog.com/articles/peptide-startups-vc-funding-legalization) (2026); [afslaw.com](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/longevity-lens/the-amino-zing-world-peptide-regulation)

### DPC + HSA
- **One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Jan 1, 2026)**: DPC monthly fees now HSA-eligible up to $150/mo individual, $300/mo family. Removes prior HSA disqualification. Major tailwind for DPC model adoption in HDHP population.
- Source: [medmoneyguide.com](https://medmoneyguide.com/guides/dpc-concierge-medicine-economics)

### AI Therapy / Mental Health
- Cerebral cautionary tale: prescription metric gamification → DEA/DOJ → $6.6M+ settlement
- AI therapy AI (chatbots) face FDA classification questions; no approved standalone AI therapy product as of 2026
- Telehealth DEA prescribing rules: temporary COVID flexibilities ended; Schedule II/III now require in-person (or video with state-by-state variation) before Rx
