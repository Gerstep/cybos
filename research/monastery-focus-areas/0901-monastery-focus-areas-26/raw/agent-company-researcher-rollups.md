## Business Model (market-level)

Three distinct business models are competing under the "AI roll-up" banner. Monastery should treat them as different asset classes, not one trade:

1. **Buy-then-AI (majority pattern)**: Raise venture capital, acquire a fragmented, labor-intensive services business (accounting firm, MSP, call center, HOA manager, letting agency), then retrofit AI onto the acquired P&L. General Catalyst's Creation portfolio (Accrual, Beacon, Crescendo, Dwelly, Eudia, Integral, Long Lake, Titan MSP), Thrive Holdings (Current, Shield Technology Partners), Beacon Software, Metropolis, Long Lake, AGI, Arca Wealth, Legora, VetnCare all fit here. Capital requirement is large ($75M–$2B) because you are financing acquisitions, not just software. This is **not Monastery-shaped** — it needs $100M+ per General Catalyst's own account (usesonicai.com, 2026).
2. **AI-then-buy / AI-native services firm from day one**: Found the company as an operating services business (not a software vendor) that never had a pre-AI cost structure to disrupt. Crosby (law firm), Tenex (MDR/SOC), Comeryx/FutureProof/AGI (MGAs), Arca (RIA). Capital needs are smaller at inception ($5–60M seed/Series A) because there's no acquisition price to pay — this is closer to Monastery-shaped, though most of these have already raised well past seed.
3. **SaaS selling into the vertical** (the pattern Monastery explicitly does NOT want unless it's a wedge to #2): Fieldguide, Klarity/Within, Adaptive, Rogo, V7, EvenUp, Sierra, Decagon, Sage (senior care), Probook, Instinct Science/Vetspire/Lupa (vet), Overjet/Pearl/VideaHealth (dental). These sell software/subscriptions to incumbent service providers rather than owning the service P&L. Several (EvenUp, Klarity, Adaptive) explicitly describe partnering with an outsourced-services firm (LLŪM for Adaptive) as a step toward owning delivery — worth watching as a wedge.

## Product/Service Notes by Vertical

- **Accounting/tax**: Thrive's Current (~30 firms, 2,000+ employees, $500M+ revenue, Tax AI cut prep time ~31%, per Jupid 2026-08 and Forbes 2026-06-02) and GC's Accrual ($75M raised) are the clearest buy-then-AI plays. Multiplier Holdings ($62.5M, Ribbit/Lightspeed) and Modus (Lightspeed/Comma/Garry Tan, ~$85M) are smaller entrants. Ascend (Alpine Investors, $590M revenue, 63 offices) and Mercer Advisors/Modern Wealth-style platforms are traditional PE roll-ups adding an AI layer after the fact — the "hybrid buyer" category per Jupid's Aug-2026 taxonomy, not AI-native by design.
- **Legal**: Two structurally different plays — Legora (formerly Leya, $5.6B valuation per legora.com 2026-07, 5 acquisitions in 2026: Cadastral, Wexler, Walter AI, Qura, Graceview) is buying AI-native point-solution startups to build a platform, still selling software to law firms (SaaS). Crosby ($85.8M total raised across Seed/A/B, $60M Series B 2026-03-31, Lux/Index/Sequoia/Elad Gil) and Eudia ($105M raised, acquired Johnson Hana 2025-07) are actual law firms/legal-service P&L owners charging per-contract or subscription fees instead of billable hours — closer to the Monastery-relevant model. Harvey remains SaaS (skip per brief) despite an active M&A/acqui-hire program (indianlegaltech.net, 2026).
- **MSP/IT services**: Titan MSP (GC, $74M, acquired RFA, targets 30%+ workflow automation per inforcapital.com 2025-08-14) and Evergreen Services Group (100–120+ MSPs, ~$1.5B revenue, ~$250M EBITDA per channelholic.news 2026, targeting 30–40 acquisitions in 2026 per CRN) are the two poles: GC's is AI-platform-first, Evergreen's is decentralized "let 100 MSPs experiment" plus a new AI-native incubation (Raiz, an AI-native ERP VAR under Pine Services Group, launched April 2026). Thrive's Shield Technology Partners (~9 firms as of Feb 2026, doubling planned in 2026) is the accounting-adjacent MSP arm.
- **CX/call center**: Confirms the brief's hypothesis — this is now crowded and largely **too late** for a Monastery-stage wedge. Sierra ($15.8B valuation, $200M ARR, ~79x revenue multiple per Sacra/CODEW 2026), Decagon ($4.5B valuation, Jan 2026), and incumbents Concentrix/Teleperformance (both facing "uninvestable" stock narratives per Bloomberg 2026-06-30 as AI erodes their core headcount-billing model) dominate. Crescendo (GC's own vertically-integrated call-center rollup, $500M valuation post-PartnerHero acquisition, 90% ticket auto-resolution) is the one genuine buy-then-AI play in this vertical and is itself a GC Creation company — i.e., already claimed by a $1.5B-fund player, not a $2M seed opportunity.
- **Insurance MGA/brokerage**: A live, less-crowded vertical. Comeryx ($7.5M seed, Feb 2026, E&S small-commercial), FutureProof (AI underwriting + Terrafuse AI catastrophe-analytics acquisition, E&S property, May 2026), and AGI/American Growth Insurance (~$70M, buy-then-AI brokerage rollup, reports ~50% profit lift across 10 pilot agencies per SiliconANGLE 2026-07-15) show three sub-models: build-from-scratch MGA, acquire-and-automate brokerage, and AI-augmented underwriting engine. Newfront (tech-enabled broker, acquired by WTW for $1.05B–$1.3B, Jan 2026) is the exit comp showing incumbents will pay ~12x EBITDA for AI-native distribution — but also shows the ceiling: WTW absorbed it rather than letting it compound independently.
- **Property/HOA/real estate**: Long Lake (GC/Alpha Wave/Thrive Capital, 30+ acquisitions, HOA volume growth from 0–5%/yr to 20%+/yr, $100M EBITDA in <2 years, then the $6.3B Amex GBT take-private in 2026 — PitchBook calls this GC's "first AI take-private") and Dwelly (UK lettings, $93M+ raised, 8+ agencies acquired, resolution time cut from 50 to 20 days per capitalandclarity.substack.com) are the flagship examples. Both are GC-adjacent and capital-heavy — not Monastery-shaped, but instructive on what "wedge → platform" looks like at scale.
- **Home services**: Split between AI-only SaaS (Probook, $40M a16z/Sequoia, dispatch AI, does NOT own the P&L) and roll-ups that are thin on AI (PipeDreams, $25.5M, HVAC/plumbing acquisitions, debt-funded, AI maturity unclear from public sources) and large incumbent franchise consolidators (Neighborly — no confirmed AI-native transformation found in this pass; treat as a "too-late incumbent," not researched to the same depth this round). This vertical looks **under-served by genuine AI-native P&L owners** — flag as a candidate white space for Monastery-stage wedges (dispatch/scheduling ops with a services arm, not just a SaaS tool).
- **Veterinary**: Mostly SaaS/practice-management (Instinct Science + ScribbleVet acquisition, Vetspire post-Thrive-Pet-Healthcare spinout with Battery Ventures backing, Lupa in the UK). VetnCare (backed by Great Point Partners, 7 acquisitions including 2 in 6 weeks per theunderbite.co 2026-08-14) is the one actual clinic-ownership roll-up found, but AI-native intensity is not yet publicly disclosed — needs a follow-up pass.
- **Dental DSO**: This is now "every major DSO has an AI vendor" (dsonews.ai 2026 report) — Overjet, Pearl, VideaHealth sell imaging/diagnostic AI into Heartland Dental, Aspen Dental/PDS Health, Dental Care Alliance. All of this is **SaaS-into-incumbent-DSO**, not AI-native DSO ownership. No AI-native, AI-owned DSO roll-up was found in this pass — flag as unclaimed vertical or note that the DSO PE ownership structure (dental-specific state laws restricting corporate practice of medicine) may structurally block the buy-then-AI model that works in accounting/legal/MSP.
- **Wealth management/RIA**: Arca Wealth (GC/Index/Venrock, $64M total, $1B+ AUM via 2 RIA acquisitions — Sandbox Financial, Granite Bay — explicitly modeled on GC's Creation strategy per RIABiz 2026-06-27, which also raises the "just PE with an AI narrative" critique) is the AI-native entrant. Mercer Advisors/Modern Wealth are traditional PE roll-ups (Altas Partners, Crestview) without a confirmed AI-native architecture claim as strong as Arca's.
- **Asset management (large-cap transformation, not Monastery-relevant but instructive)**: Percepta (GC's enterprise-transformation company) embedding inside Janus Henderson (Claude-powered Prism/Libros tools, June 2026) following GC/Trian's $7.6B Janus Henderson take-private — this is the "GC playbook applied to a public company" pattern, capital scale far beyond Monastery.
- **Adjacent but not roll-ups — AI labor/data-training marketplaces**: Mercor ($2B annualized gross revenue by June 2026, in talks for $20B valuation, ~30,000 contractor pool, per Sacra/Bloomberg 2026-07) and Mechanize ($9.1M seed at $500M valuation, then reported $1.5B+ Google licensing/acqui-hire talks by Aug 2026) are not services roll-ups — they are labor/RL-environment marketplaces for training frontier models. Included because they show where "AI-native labor" capital is actually flowing (into training data, not service delivery), and because Invisible Technologies ($134M 2024 revenue, $2B+ valuation, profitable, 11% EBITDA margin per Sacra) sits at the boundary — it is a hybrid AI-infrastructure-plus-human-workforce vendor to enterprises, not a vertical-service P&L owner.

💡 **Key Insights**:
- The prior Oct-2025 thesis (call-center AI roll-up) is now confirmed **crowded and largely too late** for a $2M/seed wedge: Sierra and Decagon are >$4B-$15B valued SaaS incumbents, and GC already owns the buy-then-AI lane via Crescendo. Concentrix/Teleperformance's stock crash (Jun 2026) is the market repricing the whole category, not an opportunity signal.
- The **buy-then-AI model structurally requires $75M-$2B+ of capital** (GC's own stated $100-150M per portfolio company) — this is fundamentally a PE/growth-equity asset class wearing venture branding, not a Monastery-stage ($2M, 12-week) opportunity. Monastery's real angle, if any, is at the **AI-then-buy / organic AI-native service** model (Crosby, Tenex, Comeryx-style MGAs) where the first product is real service delivery from day one and capital scales with proven unit economics rather than an acquisition war chest.
- **Under-automated, less-crowded verticals** worth deeper Monastery-specific scoping: home services (dispatch/ops-heavy, PipeDreams/Probook show demand but no strong AI-native P&L owner yet), veterinary practice ownership (VetnCare exists but AI-native depth unconfirmed), and insurance MGA/brokerage (still only 3-4 real entrants: Comeryx, FutureProof, AGI).
- Dental DSO looks structurally blocked for a venture-stage AI-native ownership play (corporate-practice-of-medicine restrictions push AI into a vendor role — Overjet/Pearl/VideaHealth — rather than ownership), a useful negative case for the framework.
- Watch the "SaaS → services wedge" companies (Adaptive's LLŪM partnership, EvenUp's move into pre-litigation funding, Klarity's rebrand to Within/"Company Brain") — several vertical-SaaS companies are visibly circling the buy-then-AI model without committing yet.

✅ **Strengths of the roll-up category (for GP debate)**:
- Multiple companies show real operating proof points, not just hype: Long Lake ($100M EBITDA in <2 years, HOA growth 0-5%→20%+ organically), Invisible Technologies (profitable, 11% EBITDA margin on $134M revenue), Crescendo (90% ticket auto-resolution, 4x margins vs. traditional BPO).
- Category has produced two public-market-scale deals in 2026 (Long Lake/Amex GBT $6.3B; GC-Trian/Janus Henderson $7.6B), proving the model can execute at real scale, not just seed-stage press releases.

⚠️ **Concerns**:
- Valuation/reflexivity risk: Sierra at ~79x ARR, Mercor's gross-vs-net revenue conflation ($2B gross vs. $600-800M net), and the OpenAI/Thrive circular-investment structure (flagged by Bloomberg/TechCrunch as part of the "AI bubble debate") all suggest parts of this market are priced on narrative, not disclosed unit economics.
- "Anti-private-equity" framing (Beacon, Long Lake) is doing a lot of rhetorical work; several of these vehicles use the same acquisition-multiple-arbitrage mechanics as traditional roll-ups, just funded by VC instead of PE and with AI as the promised margin lever — the Thrive Holdings piece itself notes "the arbitrage that makes the first fifty acquisitions attractive is exactly what makes the next two hundred expensive" (recatools.com, 2026-08-16).
- Public information is thin on actual AI-driven margin improvement net of integration cost for most of these companies; most disclosed metrics (EBITDA doubling, margin quadrupling) come from company or investor press releases, not audited financials — treat as directional, not verified.
- Several "AI-native" claims (Ascend/Alpine, PipeDreams, VetnCare) are unverifiable from public sources as to how deep the AI actually runs vs. traditional PE roll-up with an AI marketing layer — this is exactly the critique RIABiz raised about Arca Wealth.
