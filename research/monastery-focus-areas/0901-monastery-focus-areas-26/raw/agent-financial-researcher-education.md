## Market-level funding context (macro, cite before company detail)

- **Global EdTech VC funding, H1 2026: ~$1.0B, down 26% YoY** from $1.35B in H1 2025. Deal *count* held roughly flat — the contraction is in average deal size (mega-rounds disappeared), not deal frequency. East Asia (+37% deal volume) and Early Childhood Education (deal count more than tripled YoY) are the two areas bucking the pullback. — HolonIQ, "1B in EdTech Venture Capital for 1H," Jul 2026 (https://www.holoniq.com/notes/1b-in-edtech-venture-capital-to-date-funding-falls-short-of-last-years-midpoint-asia-mena-buck-the-trend)
- **Full-year trend:** 2020 $16.1B → 2021 $20.8B (peak) → 2022 $10.6B → 2023 $3.0B → 2024 $2.3B → 2025 $2.6B (+11% YoY, "market stabilizes"). 2026 is *not* continuing that recovery on a YoY basis. — HolonIQ 2026 Global Education Outlook (https://www.holoniq.com/notes/2026-global-education-outlook); 36Kr summary of HolonIQ data, Jul 2026 (https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3903992759568262)
- **Narrower "pure-play EdTech" tracker:** Jan–Jul 2026 = ~$435M across 24 deals vs. ~$774M across 37 deals in the same window of 2025. Average round size down from $20.9M to $18.1M; median down from $5.2M to $4.7M. Ex-rounds->$50M, YTD 2026 capital is only ~$160M — i.e., the market is a handful of big rounds sitting on top of a thin base of small ones. — New Market Pitch, "EdTech Funding Trends (2026)" (https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/edtech-funding-trends)
- **AI is now most of the category, not a subcategory:** AI-focused education startups raised **$4.2B in 2025 = 62% of all edtech VC funding**; AI tutors + educator-copilot tools alone captured ~85% of that $4.2B. >2,800 "AI education" startups now exist, an 18x increase since 2023. — ValueAdd VC synthesis citing HolonIQ/Crunchbase data, 2026 (https://valueaddvc.com/blog/ai-tutors-are-here-how-duolingo-khan-academy-and-startups-are-personalizing-education)
- **Sentiment at ASU+GSV 2026 (the industry's largest annual gathering, San Diego):** "cap tables feel tight," generalist investors remain wary of edtech, several edtech-focused VCs have not delivered promised returns to LPs, no one expects a healthy M&A market or IPO window in the next 12 months. Investors are shifting from B2C subscription bets toward "workflow-embedded," outcomes-evidenced, and services/tech-enabled-services models. — Edtech Insiders Substack, "The Edtech Insiders Rundown of ASU+GSV 2026" (https://edtechinsiders.substack.com/p/the-edtech-insiders-rundown-of-asugsv-55b); Tyton Partners, ASU+GSV 2026 recap (https://tytonpartners.com/asugsv-2026-what-we-heard-in-our-conversations-and-between-the-lines/)
- **Bottleneck data point the sector is now organizing around:** Khan Academy's Khanmigo grew from ~40,000 to ~700,000 active student users in one school year (731% YoY signup growth) once Microsoft made it free for every US teacher (May 2024) — but **only ~15% of students with access actually use it regularly**. Free distribution ≠ engagement; this is becoming the sector's benchmark objection. — ValueAdd VC, 2026 (same URL as above); Khan Academy SY24-25 Annual Report (https://annualreport.khanacademy.org/)

## Failures / downrounds / distressed situations

### BYJU'S (Think & Learn) — the sector's largest cautionary tale
- Peak valuation **$22B** (Oct 2022, $250M round incl. Qatar Investment Authority).
- BlackRock (a <1% holder) marked its stake down implying **~$11B** by Apr 2023, then further to **~$1B** by early 2024 — a ~95% markdown *by a single passive investor's own marks*, well before insolvency.
- Built via aggressive, debt-fueled M&A during the pandemic bump: Osmo ($120M, 2019), WhiteHat Jr ($300M, 2020), Aakash Educational Services (~$950M–$3B, 2021), Epic! ($500M, 2021), Great Learning + Toppr ($600M+, 2021), Gradeup + Tynker ($200M, 2021), GeoGebra ($100M, 2021) — then a **$1.2B term loan (Nov 2021)** from US lenders became the trigger: delayed FY21 audited financials (17 months late, released Sep 2022) revealed losses of ₹4,588 crore, Deloitte resigned as auditor, board members exited, lenders alleged >$500M was moved without disclosure.
- US subsidiary Alpha filed Chapter 11 (Delaware) Feb 1, 2024; Indian parent Think & Learn entered NCLT insolvency Jul 16, 2024 (triggered by a relatively small ₹158cr BCCI sponsorship dispute).
- **As of Sep 1, 2026 (this research's "as of" date): still unresolved.** NCLT has repeatedly paused the Form-G bidder-invitation process amid a founder challenge to GLAS Trust's (US lender agent) ~₹11,433cr claim and >99% creditor-committee voting power; resolution professional has sought a further 90-day extension; founders separately fighting Singapore contempt convictions. Practical valuation is now assessed near **$0–500M** (liabilities exceed assets).
- Sources: CNBC, Mar 1, 2024 (https://www.cnbc.com/2024/03/01/the-rise-and-fall-of-byjus-once-a-startup-darling-in-india.html); Economic Times, "From $22 billion to zero" (https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/company/corporate-trends/from-22-billion-to-zero-success-not-failure-brought-byju-down/articleshow/131345574.cms); NCLT proceedings coverage, The Hindu BusinessLine / Economic Times, Aug 2026 (https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/in-relief-to-founders-nclt-stays-byjus-form-g-bidding-process-till-next-hearing/article71260995.ece; https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/startups/byjus-resolution-professional-seeks-90-day-extension-to-resolve-insolvency/articleshow/133316792.cms).

## Typical seed–A valuations: AI tutors (consumer/B2C) vs. school SaaS (B2B/district)

**Consumer AI tutor / language / K-12 subject-tutor apps (B2C or B2B2C via parents):**
- Pre-seed: $3–8M raised at implied ~$10–25M post (Sparkli $5M pre-seed; Wippi $1.2M seed; Giant $8M seed; Wild Zebra $6M seed on $8M total raised).
- Seed: $2–8M typical check, $8–25M post typical for a team with early usage traction but pre-revenue-scale (Medly AI $8M seed following an earlier £1.7M pre-seed; Class Companion $4M seed).
- Series A: $15–25M raised, $80–450M post depending on user/revenue scale — wide range because "Series A" for consumer AI-tutor apps is priced on engagement/DAU trajectory more than ARR: SchoolAI ($25M/$84M implied), Brisk Teaching ($15M/$83.5M implied), Gizmo ($22M, undisclosed but 13M users), MagicSchool ($15.3M Series A/$100M post in 2024, jumping to $431M post seven months later at Series B on the strength of "6M educators signed up").
- Breakout consumer AI tutors reach unicorn status fast off ARR, not district contracts: **Speak hit $1B in 30 months from launch of its AI feature-set** (Series B extension $500M Jun 2024 → Series C $1B Dec 2024, on the back of $100M+ ARR reported in 2025).
- **Rule of thumb for Monastery-stage AI tutor companies: seed checks are $2–8M at $8–25M post; a $2M-into-a-$40M-post SAFE (Monastery's implied terms, see below) sits at the high end of seed / low end of "hot seed" for this category** — reasonable only for a team with early usage proof (schools piloting, or a working demo with retention data), not a bare idea.

**B2B school/district SaaS (LMS, teacher-copilot, assessment infra sold into districts):**
- Seed: $5–15M typical, since sales cycles (6–12 months, RFP-heavy) demand more runway before first ACV lands; MagicSchool's early Series A-4 (Aug 2023) priced at just **$13.89M post** off a $2.42M raise, backed by **Reach Capital, GSV Ventures, Rethink Education, Charter School Growth Fund** — i.e., a genuinely early school-SaaS company can be primed well under $40M post even with name-brand edtech VCs already in.
- Series A: $15–25M raised at $80–100M post typical when there's a credible district pilot base (SchoolAI, Brisk, MagicSchool Series A all cluster in this band).
- District SaaS valuations expand more slowly than consumer AI tutor valuations at the same funding stage because growth is gated by district budget cycles (6–12 month sales cycles, RFP-heavy, ESSER/E-Rate funding-cliff exposure) rather than app-store virality.
- **A district-facing school-SaaS company priced at $40M post pre-revenue or pre-pilot would be aggressive relative to comps; the same valuation is defensible if the company already has signed district pilots or a state-level deal (cf. Synthesis Tutor's statewide Oklahoma rollout, MagicSchool's "6M educators, thousands of districts").**

## Unit economics: parent LTV (B2C) vs. district ACV (B2B)

**B2C / parent-paid subscription:**
- Directional B2C EdTech ACV benchmark: **$50–500/year** per household; LTV:CAC ratio benchmark **2:1–4:1**; CAC payback **6–18 months**. — SaaSHero, "EdTech Marketing Metrics: The Essential 2026 Guide" (https://www.saashero.net/strategy/edtech-marketing-metrics-2026/)
- Real examples: Praktika reported **~$20M trailing-12-month revenue off 1.2M MAU** at its May-2024 Series A (implying low-teens-$/user/year blended, consistent with freemium-to-paid conversion economics); Speak crossed **$100M ARR in 2025**, the first AI-language product to do so, at a subscription price point in the same consumer range.
- Duolingo (the scale benchmark) monetizes ~9% of its 140.6M MAU into 12.7M paid subscribers — **a ~9% paid-conversion rate is the standing high-water mark** most consumer AI-tutor seed decks should be checked against; free-tier-to-paid conversion in K-12 specifically runs only ~3–7% per SaaS benchmarks above.
- Structural risk: B2C parent-paid apps face high organic-vs-paid CAC volatility and are directly exposed to Big Tech giving away comparable free tools (Google's free "Guided Learning" in Gemini, Aug 2025; OpenAI/Anthropic free study tools cited by Wild Zebra as a competitive pressure it is explicitly underwriting against).

**B2B / district ACV:**
- District ACV bands by tier (per K-12 EdTech revenue-architecture benchmarks, 2026/2027 modeling):

| Tier | District size | ACV band | Sales motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 Enterprise | 25,000+ students (~365 districts: NYC DOE, LAUSD, Miami-Dade, Houston ISD, etc.) | $150K – $4M | Named-account field AE |
| Tier 2 Mid-Market | 2,500–25,000 students (~3,200 districts) | $35K – $150K | Territory inside AE |
| Tier 3 SMB/Charter | <2,500 students (~9,700 districts + charters) | $4K – $35K | Inside sales / self-serve + SDR |

- Per-student-per-year (PSPY) pricing standards: $6–14 for supplemental literacy/math (i-Ready, IXL, Lexia-class products); $12–28 for core curriculum; $35–80 for SIS/LMS bundles (PowerSchool, Infinite Campus-class); $95–180 for assessment/analytics suites (Renaissance, NWEA MAP-class).
- B2B LTV:CAC benchmark **3:1–6:1**; CAC payback **12–24 months** (materially longer than B2C, consistent with 6–12 month district sales cycles) — formula: LTV:CAC = (ACV × Gross Margin % × avg. contract length in years) ÷ CAC. — pulserevops.com, "Revenue Architecture for K-12 EdTech" (https://pulserevops.com/revenue-architecture/ra0051)
- Structural risk specific to district ACV: **ESSER/E-Rate pandemic-relief funding cliff** — the ~$190B ARPA ESSER pool was fully obligated by Sep 2024, meaning district budgets that funded a lot of 2021–2024 edtech seat growth have now reverted to baseline; new district SaaS ACV growth in 2025–2026 is coming from actual line-item budget, not stimulus, which is one reason the market treats district-validated companies (state pilots, multi-district signed contracts) as de-risked relative to consumer apps.
- **Net comparison: parent-paid LTV is small ($50–500/yr per household, thin gross-dollar retention, high paid-media CAC volatility) but scales instantly and globally; district ACV is large per logo ($4K–$4M) but slow to land (6–12 month sales cycles) and politically/budget-cycle fragile.** The Monastery-relevant read: a $2M check buys ~12–24 months of runway for either model, but a B2C team needs to show real paid-conversion (not just downloads) in that window, while a B2B team needs at least one signed district or state pilot to justify a follow-on Series A.

## Monastery lens

**Deal math:** Public terms are **$2M uncapped SAFE + 5% equity**, seed–Series A stage, 12 weeks. Two implied readings:
1. **If read as a priced-equivalent 5% stake for $2M**, that implies a **~$40M post-money** valuation reference point (2M / 0.05).
2. **As actually structured (uncapped SAFE + separate 5% equity grant)**, Monastery is not pricing the company at all — it is buying 5% of the cap table outright (likely via a low-value common/founder-stock grant tied to program participation) *plus* extending $2M of uncapped SAFE capital that converts at whatever the company's next priced round says. This is materially founder-friendlier than a capped SAFE at $40M, and structurally different from a typical seed lead — it behaves more like **accelerator equity (YC-style, but at 100x YC's historical check size) stacked on top of uncapped bridge capital**.

**Where a $2M check (uncapped SAFE) + 5% fits against the comps above:**
- **Too big relative to true pre-seed/idea-stage rounds** in this sector — Sparkli ($5M pre-seed at Founderful), Wippi ($1.2M seed), Giant ($8M seed with three co-leads) show that pre-traction consumer-AI-education teams typically raise the *whole* pre-seed/seed round at $2–8M total from a syndicate, not as a single $2M check from one accelerator-style investor. A Monastery $2M check could plausibly *be* the entire pre-seed/seed round for a scrappy 2-person AI-tutor team, which is exactly the profile Monastery is built for.
- **Reasonable relative to "hot seed" consumer AI-tutor rounds** — MagicSchool's Series A ($15.3M/$100M post, Jun 2024) and SchoolAI's Series A ($25M/$84M implied, Apr 2025) show $2M as a *component* of a syndicate seed extension is normal; Monastery's $2M would typically be a piece of, not the entirety of, a Series A-adjacent raise.
- **Too small relative to district-validated school SaaS Series A** ($15–46M rounds: SchoolAI, Brisk, Campus) — a Monastery-stage company selling into districts would need Monastery's check as bridge/pre-seed capital before a "real" Series A from Owl/GSV/Reach/Bessemer-class investors, not as the main round.
- **The uncapped SAFE structure is the key protection**: since it doesn't fix a $40M price, Monastery isn't overpaying if the company is objectively pre-seed-stage (most of the 100-company universe in this sector is); it captures upside if the company prices its next round meaningfully above $40M (plausible for anything that reaches Speak/MagicSchool/SchoolAI-style traction within 12 months of the program).

**Which company *profiles* at seed–A could plausibly take a $2M Monastery check (illustrative, based on comps found — not a solicitation list):**
- **Consumer AI-tutor / language / subject-tutor apps pre-Series-A**, team of 2–10, some usage/retention data but not yet at $1–5M ARR — profile matches Medly AI (pre-£1.7M-round stage), Wild Zebra (pre-$6M-round stage), Sparkli (pre-$5M-round stage), Class Companion (pre-$4M-round stage). These are exactly the check sizes ($4–8M) that a $2M Monastery allocation could meaningfully seed or extend.
- **Early childhood / parenting / child-development AI products** — this is the one subsegment HolonIQ flags as *tripling* in deal count YoY even as the broader market contracts (Giant, Wippi, Sparkli, AnswersNow-adjacent). Monastery-stage checks here are proportionally more impactful because round sizes are smaller ($1.2–8M) and the investor base (12 Flags, Founderful, Matrix/Decasonic) is thinner/less specialized than in K-12 SaaS, meaning Monastery's SF-based AI-native operator network is a differentiated value-add, not a commodity check.
- **NOT a good fit at $2M**: district-facing school SaaS requiring 6–12 month sales cycles and $150K+ ACVs before any revenue signal — these need dedicated-edtech-VC-sized seed rounds ($5–15M) and multi-year runway before a district contract lands; a 12-week accelerator sprint does not compress a school procurement cycle. **NOT a good fit at $2M**: anything already at Series-B-plus valuation working through legacy debt/distress (BYJU'S, Chegg, 2U-style turnarounds) — wrong stage and wrong problem (financial engineering, not 12-week product velocity) for Monastery's model.
