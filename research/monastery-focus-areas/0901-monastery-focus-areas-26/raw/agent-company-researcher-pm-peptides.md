## REGULATORY LAYER: FDA 503A vs 503B — The 2025–2026 Crackdown

### What Happened

The entire compounded GLP-1 industry was built on a shortage-list loophole. Timeline:

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| 2022 | Semaglutide + tirzepatide added to FDA shortage list → 503A + 503B compounding permitted |
| Late 2022–2024 | Mass-market compounded GLP-1 industry explodes: telehealth + pharmacy chains |
| Dec 2024 | FDA declares tirzepatide shortage resolved → 503B wind-down clock starts |
| Feb 2025 | FDA declares semaglutide shortage resolved → 503A grace period starts |
| Apr 22, 2025 | 503A enforcement discretion for semaglutide "essentially a copy" compounding ends |
| May 22, 2025 | 503B enforcement discretion for semaglutide ends |
| Sep–Oct 2025 | FDA sends 50+ warning letters to telehealth GLP-1 providers |
| Feb 2026 | FDA refers Hims & Hers to DOJ; signals API restriction crackdown |
| Mar 2026 | Hims settles with Novo Nordisk; stops advertising compounded GLP-1 |
| Apr 1, 2026 | FDA reiterates: 503A limited safe harbor of ≤4 Rxes/month of copy only |
| Apr 30, 2026 | FDA proposes **permanently excluding** semaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutide from 503B bulks list |
| May 13, 2026 | Strive Pharmacy receives FDA closeout letter (prior warning resolved) |
| Jul 23–24, 2026 | FDA PCAC votes to recommend BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax, Epitalon for 503A bulks list — **non-binding** |
| Aug 2026 | 135+ total GLP-1 warning letters issued; proposed rule comment period closed Jun 29 |

### 503A vs 503B: The Key Distinction

**503A (traditional compounding pharmacy)**
- State-licensed pharmacy or physician
- Must compound patient-specific prescriptions (individual identified patient + valid Rx)
- "Essentially a copy" standard: in general, cannot mass-produce copies of FDA-approved drugs
- Safe harbor: ≤4 Rxes/month of a copy product (Apr 2026 clarification)
- Regulated primarily by state boards of pharmacy
- **Still operating** for patient-specific compounding

**503B (outsourcing facility)**
- FDA-registered facility, subject to cGMP manufacturing standards
- Can produce large batches for office stock without patient-specific Rx
- Cannot compound semaglutide, tirzepatide, or liraglutide as of Apr 2026 (off shortage list)
- Proposed rule (Apr 30, 2026) would permanently block bulks-list pathway for GLP-1s
- **Pathway for GLP-1 mass production is effectively closed**

## EU PEPTIDE REGULATORY LANDSCAPE

**Summary**: Strict but complex. No viable compounding pathway for research peptides. Gray market persists.

| Country | BPC-157 / TB-500 compounding | Personal possession | Criminal risk |
|---------|------------------------------|--------------------|----|
| Germany | Prohibited (AMG) | Not explicitly criminalised | §73 AMG import = fines; active customs enforcement |
| Austria | Similar to Germany | Not criminalised | AMG-AT equivalent |
| UK (post-Brexit) | Prohibited (Human Medicines Regs 2012) | Not criminal | MHRA ignores "research use" labels; moderate enforcement |
| Netherlands | Prohibited | Not criminalised | Lower customs priority |
| France/Italy/Spain | Prohibited | Varies | Lower enforcement |

**Key 2026 development**: EMA synthetic-peptide guideline (applicable Jun 2026) — drug-manufacturing guidance for EU approvals. Creates no new requirement for buyers; but defines peptide strength by base mass (not salt form) — relevant for dosing accuracy standards.

**Magistral compounding**: EU Directive 2001/83/EC exempts magistral (patient-specific) compounding but only for recognized pharmaceutical starting materials with agreed quality specs. BPC-157, TB-500, GHK-Cu, MOTS-c, ipamorelin have **no EU marketing authorisation, no European drug-quality entry** — cannot lawfully be compounded as magistral formulations.

Sources: [DOSED Germany guide 2026](https://dosed.pro/blog/bpc-157-legal-germany-2026.html); [PeptidesDNA Europe 2026](https://www.peptidesdna.com/learn/peptide-pharmacy-europe-2026)

## MONASTERY FILTER: $2M SEED / 12-WEEK AI-NATIVE COMPANY

### Can a $2M seed own a 503A/clinic network, OR only software into it?

**Answer**: $2M cannot own a 503A pharmacy at typical valuations; it can own a **small seed-stage telehealth + software platform** that integrates with licensed pharmacies.

| Pathway | $2M feasibility | 12-week AI-native leverage | Path to $1B+ |
|---------|----------------|--------------------------|------|
| **Build a 503A pharmacy** | No. Cleanroom buildout alone $500K–$2M; USP 797/800 compliance 12–18 months; state licensure multi-state is years of work | No | Not a $1B path for a single pharmacy |
| **Acquire a 503A pharmacy** | Borderline. Small 503A ($500K SDE) trades 3.5–5.5x = $1.75–3M. $2M seed + SBA 70% = feasible structure, but regulatory diligence risk | Operational, not AI-leverage | Fragmented; roll-up potential if platform |
| **Noom/Tailor Made roll-up model** | Requires $50M+ scale. Noom had $3.7B valuation when it acquired TMC. Not seed-appropriate | N/A | Yes — if funded |
| **Software into clinic/pharmacy** | Yes. Clinical AI copilot, intake automation, protocol generation, biomarker interpretation — all software-only, sub-$2M build | High | Depends on contract capture vs. data moat |
| **AI-native telehealth platform (Hone/Maximus model)** | Yes. Maximus is bootstrapped-to-VC; Hone raised $6M pre-Series A. $2M is enough to get to ramen ARR with telehealth + 503A supplier | High | Yes if expanding beyond single condition |
| **Diagnostic lab + AI (Function model)** | Near-impossible at $2M (Quest contracts, phlebotomy network). Function raised $800M+ | N/A | Already crowded |
| **Premium offline clinic (Fountain Life model)** | No. Single location = $2–5M capex; not AI-leveraged at seed | No | Requires PE capital |

### Most Viable Monastery Path:
1. **AI-native telehealth + 503A supplier relationship**: Build the patient-facing AI + clinical workflow; white-label a licensed 503A compounder. As scale grows, acquire or JV with the pharmacy. Hone Health and Maximus both started this way. $2M is enough to reach ~5,000 patients before needing Series A.
2. **Vertical AI copilot for clinic operators**: Clinical protocol AI, biomarker interpretation, patient engagement for longevity clinics (Next Health, Fountain Life, Restore). SaaS into fragmented clinic operators — but risk is low contract values and replacement by bigger players.
3. **Peptide-specific telehealth after PCAC rulemaking**: If FDA finalizes 503A bulks list for BPC-157/TB-500 (12–18 months), a Maximus-style peptide telehealth platform focused on injury recovery, gut health, or performance becomes viable. **Window is 2027–2028 assuming rule finalization.**

- 503A/clinic roll-up at scale: $100M+ capital; 3–5 year integration
- Restore-style franchise roll-up: PE-backed ($140M General Atlantic)
