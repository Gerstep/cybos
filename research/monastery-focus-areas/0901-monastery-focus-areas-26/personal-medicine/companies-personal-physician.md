# Company Catalog: AI-Native Personal Physician, Concierge Medicine, Membership Primary Care, Hybrid Offline Clinics

**Date**: 2026-09-01  
**Purpose**: Monastery investment context — map the landscape, identify whitespace for seed–A companies  
**Total companies cataloged**: 43  

---

## CRITICAL DISTINCTION

> **Owns the license and the visit** = clinician employed or contracted; bills insurance or collects membership directly; bears clinical liability; can prescribe, diagnose, treat. **INVESTABLE** (if AI-native).  
> **Sells a copilot to doctors** = SaaS into existing health systems; does not own the patient relationship; revenue from software license. **"Too-late wrapper" risk** — labeled 🔧 below.

## PATTERN ANALYSIS: WHY THINGS FAILED

### The Forward / Babylon / Carbon Cluster
1. **Hardware trap** (Forward): Building proprietary physical kiosks is a high-capex, long-lead model. CarePod = autonomous diagnosis without human = liability + churn. 3,200 kiosks planned, 5 deployed.
2. **SPAC greed + overpromised VBC** (Babylon): Went public via SPAC, needed to show growth, expanded to US + Rwanda + US Medicaid at same time; capitation risk without actuarial experience.
3. **$600M acquisition + primary care margins** (Carbon): Primary care grosses 20–30% EBITDA margin at best. Funding a $600M+ acquisition strategy with those margins requires perfect execution and scale that takes 10+ years.
4. **Retail primary care** (VillageMD + Oak Street): Walk-in patient acquisition failed; physician recruitment failed; retail co-location doesn't create the trusting patient relationship primary care requires.

### The "Copilot Wrapper" Trap
Abridge, Ambience, Nabla, OpenEvidence — all valuable and fundable — but none is the personal physician. They make existing doctors faster, not replace or augment the patient's primary care relationship. A hospital could build this internally or swap providers.

### What survived
- Insurance-integrated telehealth (Midi, Parsley) — payers create the moat
- Consumer-DTC with owned pharmacy (Hims, Ro, Numan) — margin + repeat + subscription
- Diagnostics-as-membership ($199/yr Superpower, $365/yr Function) — volume economics
- Longevity scan-as-product (Neko) — hardware moat + experience moat

## MONASTERY WHITESPACE ASSESSMENT

**Monastery filter**: $2M SAFE; seed–A; 12 weeks of AI leverage; path to $1B revenue; AI-native (not wrapper)

**Best-fit signals from this catalog**:
1. **Superpower** ($34M raised, $199/yr, 64K members, AI doctor on proprietary LLM): Closest current analog to what a Monastery company looks like in this space. Owned by founders, not PE. AI-native architecture. Consumer wedge.
2. **The gap**: Nobody yet combines (a) a personalized AI physician that (b) can also prescribe and (c) coordinates offline care (labs, imaging, specialist) at (d) $200–500/yr price point, at (e) scale without physician burnout. Superpower reaches a–d but not b (no prescribing). Function reaches a–d but no ongoing care. Neko has (c) but at $499/scan, no subscription.
3. **Regulatory moat**: In the US, prescribing requires state-licensed physician or NP. AI can support but cannot sign. Models that put a human clinician in the loop (Superpower's concierge team; Midi's 550 employed clinicians) maintain legality. Pure AI prescribing without human review = regulatory risk.
4. **The compound opportunity**: Lab network (Quest/LabCorp) + wearable data + AI physician + human concierge + prescription capability + specialist referral coordination, all wrapped in a $200–500/yr membership = the "personal physician OS" that nobody yet fully owns at seed stage.

**Monastery no-gos from this catalog**:
- Any company trying to own the physical clinic at seed stage (Forward's lesson)
- Pure clinical AI copilot SaaS (Abridge's lesson — exits as enterprise SaaS, not personal medicine)
- Women's-only specialty telehealth at seed stage (crowded; Midi/Maven/Evernow/Alloy already in market)
- Generic telehealth wrapper (Babylon's lesson)
